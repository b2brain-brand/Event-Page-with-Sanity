# Shipping an event page

Target: **30–45 minutes** per show once the venue exists. If it is taking two
hours, you are writing a blog post, not filling a template.

---

## The one rule

**Never invent a number to fill a section.**

The template is built so that a missing section costs you nothing — it
disappears along with its nav link, and the page still looks finished. A wrong
attendee count costs you the page's credibility and, once someone checks it, the
citation. Leave it empty, publish, and come back when you have the source.

---

## Before you open the Studio

Collect and paste into a scratch doc:

1. Organiser site — dates, venue, registration, pass prices, exhibitor list
2. Last year's post-show report — attendance, exhibitor count, title mix
3. Reddit / LinkedIn — search `[event name]` + "worth it", "exhibitor", "booth"
4. YouTube — recap and exhibitor-perspective videos
5. Your CRM — which of your open opportunities are on the exhibitor list

Everything you use goes in **SEO → Sources**. If a number has no source, it does
not go on the page.

---

## Order of work

Fill the tabs left to right. They are ordered so the page is publishable at the
end of tab 2 and gets richer after that.

### 1 · Hero & identity — 10 min

Name (always with the year), slug, type, dates, venue, format.

- **Venue**: pick an existing one if it is there. Creating one takes 60 seconds
  and pays for itself the third time that hall appears.
- **Hero sub-headline**: what the show is and how big, in one sentence.
- **Positioning line**: why an exhibitor should care. Not printed in the hero —
  it becomes the meta description.
- **Hero operator card**: optional. Skip it for a show you have no data on; the
  hero renders full-width and looks deliberate.

### 2 · Facts — 10 min

Four stats and the quick answer. **The page is publishable after this.**

The **quick answer** is the highest-value paragraph you will write:

> Answer in the first sentence. Dates, city, venue. Then scale. Then the one
> line about why exhibitors care. 400–600 characters.

That paragraph is what gets lifted into AI Overviews and Perplexity answers.
Write it last, after you know the show, and re-read it cold.

### 3 · Programme — 10 min

Gallery, why it matters, agenda, speakers, exhibitors.

- **Agenda**: if the schedule is not published yet, fill only **tracks**. The
  section renders with a "being confirmed" note. Come back when it drops.
- **Speakers**: 4 or 8. The ones worth knowing, not the full roster.
- **Why it matters**: two paragraphs, operator to operator. End with a customer
  pullquote carrying a before/after number if you have one.

### 4 · Audience & cost — 10 min

- **Title mix** should total roughly 100.
- **"Is your buyer here?"** must say who this show is **not** for. That sentence
  does more for trust than the rest of the page.
- **Booth cost paragraph** — a real range, and what it excludes. This wins
  "how much does it cost to exhibit at [event]".
- **ROI defaults** — set `days` to the actual show length and `meetingRate` to
  something you can defend. Visitors will change these numbers; that is the point.

### 5 · Proof & FAQ — 10 min

- **Reviews**: publish at least one **Mixed** Reddit quote. An all-positive
  block reads as marketing and gets discounted.
- **Playbook**: all three motions or none.
- **FAQ**: five questions, in this order, always —
  1. When and where is [event]?
  2. Who attends?
  3. How much does it cost to exhibit?
  4. Does it provide lead retrieval?
  5. How should an exhibitor prepare?

  Answers must be self-contained. "See above" is worthless to a crawler.
- **Similar events**: leave blank and the template auto-fills from the same
  category. Only hand-pick when you want a specific three.

### 6 · SEO & page meta — 5 min

Meta title, meta description, **last updated**, and **sources**.

---

## Voice

- Every number paired with a comparison. Never a standalone percentage.
- Third person, operator to operator. Not the founder's first person.
- No emoji, no exclamation marks, no "AI-powered".
- Banned: seamless · effortless · leverage · unlock · unleash · empower ·
  supercharge · world-class · best-in-class · next-gen · synergy · ecosystem ·
  touchpoints · journeys · experiences · boost engagement · "event tech" ·
  "lead retrieval" (as a category label) · "engagement platform".
- Own the metric: **LTM (Leads-to-Meeting)**. Spine: **Speed → Commitment → Pipeline**.

---

## Before you publish

- [ ] Every number traces to an entry in **Sources**
- [ ] Quick answer reads correctly to someone who has never heard of the show
- [ ] FAQ answer #1 restates the event name, year, dates and city
- [ ] "Is your buyer here?" names who should **not** exhibit
- [ ] At least one Mixed-tone review
- [ ] **Last updated** is today
- [ ] Checked in Presentation at desktop and mobile width

---

## After the show closes

The page does not die, it changes job — from conversion page to next-year prep.

1. Update **Last updated**.
2. Swap the gallery for photos from this edition.
3. Add exhibitor testimonials you actually collected.
4. When the next edition is announced, create the new document and add it to
   this one's **Similar events** so the ranking page hands traffic forward.

The countdown chip flips to "Past event · recap" on its own.

---

## Roll-out cadence

Do not ship 40 pages at once — that is a quality risk and it reads as one to a
crawler.

| Phase | Pages | Pick |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Pilot (wk 1–2) | 3 | Within 90 days, in the sweet-spot verticals |
| 2 — Vertical depth (wk 3–6) | +5 | Remaining Tier 1 supply chain + manufacturing |
| 3 — Adjacent (wk 7–12) | +8 | CPG / retail / pharma Tier 1 |
| 4 — Long tail | +1–2 / month | Whatever field marketing asks for |

Stop once you have covered the shows your ICP actually attends. Pages for shows
your buyers skip will not rank, and they dilute the topical authority of the
ones that do.

The Studio's **Events → Needs sourcing** list is your queue: it surfaces
anything missing a quick answer, under three FAQs, or with no
sources.
